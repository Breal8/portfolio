One entry point, six ways a card stops working.

The user says “it’s not working.” The assistant resolves which of six situations it actually is — without asking — and decides what it is allowed to do, in what order, and when it must stop. This is the architecture underneath that: the intent tree, the verification state machine, and the escalation ladder.

The claim this table stands behind — designed and documented an end-to-end conversational journey as Figma dialogue flows and version-controlled Markdown specs: a single-intent entry point with internal branching, a non-negotiable verification gate, and error-recovery states for mis-routing, failed verification, and social-engineering pressure.

01 Card card_status flag set by a rules engine — e.g. two payments, two countries, short window.
Elevated

A false unblock enables fraud; a stranded customer is abroad with no money.
Self-serve Verify Guided Human

User

Wants one thing: use my money again. Often standing at a checkout or in another country.

Assumes the assistant already knows which card and which transaction.

Assistant

Turn 1 discloses it is an assistant and its scope. States card, time, and place before any question.

Frames verification as “one quick check on our side” — the bank’s obligation, not the user’s burden. Defers the “why” until asked.

System

Holds card_last_four, transaction_amount, transaction_merchant, transaction_timestamp, country_code.

Pushes an in-app confirmation request; on approval → unblock action. No unmasked data before verification.

Failure modes

  • MIS-ROUTE Read as insufficient_funds — user is told to add money they already have.
  • VERIFY-FAIL User has no phone, or cannot approve the app request right now.
  • SOCIAL-ENG Persistence or distress used to push an unverified unblock.

Repair rule

Verification carries a “can’t do that right now” exit, framed as a pause, not a failure — it routes to a human without re-asking what was already answered.

No path where repetition, distress, or persistence produces an unverified unblock.

Invariant The bank checks itself. The user is never asked to prove they aren’t a fraudster.
02 A prior self_service_block on record, with its own timestamp.
Contained

The user already controls this action; reversing it returns them to where they were.
Self-serve Verify Guided Human

User

“It just stopped.” No fraud fear once reminded — often a small “oh, right.”

Assistant

Names the earlier self-block and its date. Offers a one-tap unblock.

No verification theatre for an action the user already owns.

System

Reads self_service_block. unblock available in-channel with no identity step.

Failure modes

  • MIS-ROUTE Sent down the bank-block flow — needless verification for a user-owned action.
  • DISPUTE User insists they never blocked it.

Repair rule

If the user disputes ever blocking the card, escalate to bank-block handling — treat the disagreement as a signal, not noise.

Invariant Don’t make the user re-earn access they never lost.
03 expiry_date is in the past.
Contained

Low risk — unless a replacement ships to a stale address.
Self-serve Verify Guided Human

User

Expects the assistant to know which card and where to send the new one.

Assistant

States the expiry date plainly. Confirms the address on file out loud rather than asking for it.

Orders the replacement; gives a delivery window in plain time, not “5–7 business days.”

System

Reads expiry_date. Address is confirmed, not displayed, until identity is checked for the reissue. order_replacement action.

Failure modes

  • VERIFY-FAIL Address on file is stale and can’t be confirmed.
  • NO-ACTION Conversation ends without the order actually placed.

Repair rule

If the address can’t be confirmed, hand to a human rather than shipping a card to an unverified address.

Invariant A replacement is an action. The turn doesn’t end until it’s placed or handed over.
04 balance < transaction_amount at time of decline.
Contained

Risk is disclosure — balance shown too early — and drifting into advice.
Self-serve Verify Guided Human

User

Believes the card is broken. May push back before the reason lands.

Assistant

States the balance and the attempted amount — after verification only. No card action, because the card is fine.

Offers no view on what the user should do about the money.

System

Reads balance vs transaction_amount. Balance masked until identity confirmed. No product or limit recommendation.

Failure modes

  • DATA-LEAK Balance surfaced before verification.
  • ADVICE-DRIFT Assistant suggests a loan, overdraft, or limit increase.

Repair rule

If the user asks what to do, name the options that exist, mark none as recommended, and offer a human for anything advisory.

Invariant The assistant states facts and performs actions. It does not advise.
05 User’s explicit statement, or emotive language treated as a slow-down signal.
Critical

Active loss in progress; re-targeting by phishers within hours is likely.
Self-serve Verify Guided Human

User

Fear of continuing loss. Wants the bleeding stopped before anything is explained.

Assistant

Blocks the card in a turn of its own — action first. Brings in a colleague for the claim.

Three consecutive turns, breaking the one-question rule on purpose. Closes with fixed anti-phishing guidance.

System

Immediate block on explicit statement or emotive signal. Opens a fraud case. Fraud-report language is fixed — designed around, not rewritten. Hard handover.

Failure modes

  • UNDER-REACT Emotive language treated as a resolved intent — no block.
  • OVER-TALK Assistant asks claim questions the human will re-ask anyway.
  • MISSING-CODA Conversation ends without the anti-phishing turn.

Repair rule

Emotive language slows the flow down, never speeds it past the block.

A conversation that ends without the anti-phishing line is incomplete and is flagged as such.

Invariant Stop the loss before discussing it. Every question here is a delay.
06 Declined once; card_status healthy; no fraud flag.
Contained

Lowest risk — the trap is a false block, or a vague ending.
Self-serve Verify Guided Human

User

Braced for a problem. Needs to hear clearly that there isn’t one.

Assistant

Confirms the card is healthy. Explains the single decline as a merchant or terminal issue. Suggests a retry.

Has good news and no action — the weakest script in the set, and the hardest to write.

System

Reads: declined once, card_status healthy, no fraud flag. No card action.

Failure modes

  • FALSE-BLOCK Assistant blocks a healthy card out of caution.
  • NO-ACTION Nothing to do, so the close drifts and reassures nobody.

Repair rule

When there is no action, the close still names one concrete thing — retry now, or come back if it repeats.

Invariant “Nothing is wrong” is a valid ending. Write it as deliberately as a fix.

The escalation ladder

Every sub-intent resolves onto one of four tiers. The assistant climbs only as far as the situation requires, and a tier is never skipped downward to keep a conversation in-channel.

S1 — Self-serve

Explain and act

User block, expired card, merchant issue. The assistant states the situation and performs the action. No identity check.

S2 — Verified action

The bank checks itself

Bank-initiated block. An in-app confirmation gates the unblock. Framed as the bank’s obligation, never an interrogation.

S3 — Guided

Facts, no advice

Insufficient funds and advisory questions. The assistant states what is true and offers a human for anything beyond facts.

S4 — Human + claim

Hard handover

Lost or stolen, failed verification, disputed history. Action first where needed, then a person. Fixed compliance language.

From research

Five think-aloud sessions — friends and colleagues aged 28–61, all Swiss retail-banking customers. Wizard-of-Oz setup, designer playing the assistant. Prompt: “Your card was just declined — open your app and get it sorted.” The single-entry-point decision and the “can’t verify right now” exit both came directly from what broke in those sessions.

Designed around

Fraud-report wording, the machine-disclosure obligation, and the no-unmasked-data-before-verification rule are fixed inputs from compliance. Toggle Mark compliance gates above to see where they constrain the flow. The conversation is built around them, not written over them.
